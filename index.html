I work at the intersection of technology and business, and what I actually do most days is product ownership: deciding what gets built next and why. At Staq I own the product backlog — running requirement sessions with stakeholders, writing the user stories and acceptance criteria engineers build from, protecting scope mid-sprint, and testing what comes back before it reaches production. The domain is payments and card products, which means requirements arrive carrying security and regulatory weight, but the discipline is the same one I would bring to any product.

A good deal of the job is investigative. When something breaks, the useful question is rarely "is this broken?" but "is this the product, the configuration, the data, or the environment?" Getting to root cause before escalating saves engineers hours they would otherwise spend chasing a problem that was never theirs. I have come to think the most valuable thing a product owner produces is often a clear, reproducible account of what actually happened.

Alongside that I am the sole developer and administrator for seven IEEE-affiliated academic conference websites, covering everything from the server up to the page. It is the part of my work with the sharpest feedback loop: when an author emails to say they cannot register for a conference, there is no ambiguity about whether something is broken.

Before Staq I was a Business Data Analysis Intern at Samsung Electronics Levant, working on MCS datasets and acting as business analyst for a sales organisation restructure on the N-ERP system. I also co-authored a peer-reviewed study in Frontiers in Psychiatry, a Q1 Scopus-indexed journal, on nurses' anxiety and attitudes toward Generative AI in clinical settings.

Beyond work I travel, and I value the perspective that comes from meeting people whose experience differs from mine. As a proud Palestinian, my heritage shapes how I think about justice, resilience and the responsibility that comes with building things.

I'm always open to new challenges and collaborations — do reach out.
